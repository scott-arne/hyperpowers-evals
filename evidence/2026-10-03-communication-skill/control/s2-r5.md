Fix the ordering properly, and decouple it from Friday.

Ship the four announced changes on schedule. Nothing in that release touches billing, so it doesn't need to carry the billing fix. Land the ordering fix on its own when the staging soak is done: if the 1.5 days holds, it rides along Friday; if it slips, it goes out the next deploy. Either way nobody has to choose between the soak and the date.

Why not the retry:

- 0 flags in 500 local runs says local commits finish inside 200 ms. It says nothing about production under load, and the nightly batch is exactly where commit latency stretches. We'd trade a 2% rate we understand for a smaller rate we can't reproduce, with a retry in the code that tells the next person it's handled.
- A retry only helps where the first `reserve()` fails visibly. If there's a path where the row already exists and `reserve()` reads the pre-`post()` state, it succeeds on stale data and there's nothing to retry on. I'd want to check the refund path for that, and check that `reserve()` is safe to call twice. That's two more things to verify before Friday, and the retry was supposed to be the cheap option.
- Once flags drop near zero the proper fix stops being urgent and the retry becomes the permanent design. A timing-dependent billing call in checkout isn't something I want to normalize.

Why waiting is cheap: reconciliation has caught every case, no money has moved, and each flag costs about 10 minutes of support time. A few more days of that is fine. What isn't fine is a compressed soak on a change that reaches into `ledger/client.py`, going out the same day as four announced features.

If the fix isn't soaked by Friday, it waits. That's a better outcome than cutting the soak.
