The release doesn't need either option. Nothing else in it touches billing, the race is already live, and it's contained: reconciliation has caught every case, no money has moved wrong, and the cost is about ten minutes of support time per flagged order. That's a known, bounded cost. The retry swaps it for an unknown one: a change to the checkout and refund paths, two days before a release marketing has announced, with no staging soak behind it. 500 local runs tell us 200 ms covers the window on a laptop. They don't tell us what the window is in the nightly batch under production load, where ledger commits are slowest. If it's longer there, we're back to flags at some new rate, now with a retry sitting in the middle of the trace. And `reserve()` isn't the flaky part; `sync.py` is calling it before the row exists. A retry patches the function that's behaving correctly.

The proper fix is 1.5 days against a two-day window, touching `ledger/client.py`, which is shared, plus a soak. That's half a day of slack. If it slips at all, we're choosing between holding the release and skipping the soak on billing code, and neither is worth it for a bug the release isn't making worse.

So: start the commit-callback fix now, on its own branch, and ship it alone after Friday. A billing change should go out by itself anyway: if anything looks off, it's the only suspect and it rolls back on its own. The retry doesn't go in at all; a timer over a race tends to stay in forever because the flags stop and the ticket closes.

One thing that would change my mind: if flag volume is high enough that support is actually hurting, give me the number and I'd consider the retry as a stopgap with a removal date attached. From what we've seen so far, it isn't.
